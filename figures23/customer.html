Motivation: Why this problem?

  • NISQ Reality: Current quantum devices are noisy; deep circuits fail due to decoherence and gate errors.
  • Scalability Issues: Existing mitigation methods like ZNE (Zero-Noise Extrapolation) require increasing circuit depth, which scales poorly.
  • The Need: We need a resource-efficient mitigation strategy specifically designed for distributed/cut circuits (QPD).

Background: NISQ & QEM

  • NISQ (Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum): 50-100 qubits, no fault tolerance yet.
  • Why Errors Matter: Noise (decoherence, crosstalk) corrupts computation results.
  • Common QEM Methods:
    • ZNE: Extrapolates to zero noise (Drawback: amplifies variance).
    • PEC: Probabilistic Cancellation (Drawback: high sampling cost).
    • Learning-based: Neural Networks (Drawback: high training cost).

Problem Statement & Goals

The Problem

Running large quantum circuits via cutting (QPD) enables execution on smaller devices, but the reconstructed fragments still suffer from inherent hardware noise.

Research Objective

To develop a localized, low-sample error mitigation framework specifically for QPD fragments that does not increase circuit depth.

Method: Quasi-Probability Decomposition (QPD)

Decomposition of CZ Gate:

Non-local gates are broken into local tensor products to fit hardware constraints.

CZ = 12 ( I ⊗ I ) + 12 ( Z ⊗ I ) + 12 ( I ⊗ Z ) − 12 ( Z ⊗ Z )

4 Variants:

  • Variant 1: I ⊗ I
  • Variant 2: Z ⊗ I
  • Variant 3: I ⊗ Z
  • Variant 4: Z ⊗ Z

Calibration: Structure-Aware Perturbation

What we do:

  • Apply small perturbations (δθ) to rotation angles.
  • Range: [-0.2, 0.2].
  • Generate K calibration circuits.

Why?

  • Preserves circuit structure (same equivalence class).
  • Avoids depth blow-up (unlike ZNE).
  • Captures local noise profile accurately.

Interpretation & Discussion

  • Validity of Linearity: The localized linear model works effectively across different QPD variants, even on real hardware.
  • Adaptability: The model successfully adapted to different noise regimes (Depolarizing in simulation vs. Coherent over-rotation on IBM Fez).
  • Cost/Benefit Analysis:
    • Benefit: Significant reduction in bias (Systematic error).
    • Cost: QPD sampling overhead remains (exponential with cuts), plus linear calibration overhead.

Limitations & Future Work

Current Limitations

  • Sampling Overhead: Scales exponentially with the number of cuts.
  • Linear Assumption: May break down under strong non-linear noise regimes.
  • Calibration Cost: Requires K runs for every circuit fragment.

Future Directions

  • Advanced Models: Explore Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) or Bayesian models for non-linear noise.
  • Dynamic Cutting: Optimize cut locations to minimize overhead.
  • Applications: Test on VQE and QAOA algorithms for larger instances.

Conclusion

  • PDR-QPD Framework: Combines structure-aware calibration with local linear correction and QPD reconstruction.
  • Validation: Successfully validated on both FakeFez simulator and IBM Fez quantum processor.
  • Impact: Achieved 54.5% error reduction on hardware, proving that localized linear learning is a viable path for NISQ error mitigation.
